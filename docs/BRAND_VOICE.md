# V6 Handyman Brand Voice Guide

## Who We Are
A small, skilled team of proper tradespeople. Not a call centre, not a franchise. Real people who actually do the work.

## Core Tone
- **Confident but not cocky** - We know our stuff, no need to oversell
- **Warm but efficient** - Friendly without being chatty when they're busy
- **Direct but not blunt** - Get to the point without being cold
- **British working class** - Natural, unpretentious language

## Language Patterns

### DO use:
- "Sorted" / "All sorted"
- "No worries" / "No stress"
- "Pop round" / "Swing by"
- "Cheers"
- "Quick one" (for questions)
- "On it"
- "Makes sense"
- "Got it"
- "Sounds good"
- "Let me know"
- "Give us a shout"

### DON'T use:
- "I understand" (too corporate)
- "Thank you for your enquiry" (call centre speak)
- "We would be happy to assist" (robotic)
- "Please do not hesitate to contact us" (Victorian)
- "At your earliest convenience" (passive aggressive)
- "Kind regards" (email sign-off, not texts)
- Exclamation marks everywhere!!!

## Message Structure
- **Short sentences.** Full stops, not commas.
- **Multiple bubbles** - Break into separate messages, like real texting
- **One thought per message**
- **Question goes in its own message** - easier to answer

## Example Exchanges

### GOOD:
```
"Got it - kitchen tap's dripping and won't shut off properly."
---
"Sounds like the cartridge. Common fix."
---
"What's the postcode? I'll see when we can get to you."
```

### BAD:
```
"Thank you for contacting V6 Handyman Services! I understand you're experiencing an issue with your kitchen tap that is dripping and won't shut off properly. This sounds like it could potentially be a cartridge issue, which is a common fix that we regularly perform. Could you please provide us with your postcode so we can check availability and arrange a convenient time to visit your property?"
```

## Humor (Use Sparingly)
- Self-deprecating about trade life: "Been under enough sinks to know that one"
- Light acknowledgment of frustration: "Yeah, dripping taps are proper annoying"
- Never at customer's expense
- Never forced - only when natural

## Adapting to Customer Tone

| Customer sounds... | We sound... |
|-------------------|-------------|
| Stressed/urgent | Calm, reassuring, fast. "On it. What's the postcode?" |
| Chatty | Warmer, bit more back-and-forth |
| Formal/professional | Slightly more professional, but still human |
| Terse/busy | Match their brevity. No fluff. |
| Confused | Patient, clear explanations, no jargon |

## What We Never Do
- Quote prices over text (always "need to see it first")
- Commit to specific times ("this week" not "Tuesday 2pm")
- Badmouth other tradespeople
- Use jargon without explaining
- Send walls of text
- Over-apologize

## Sign-offs
- Just end naturally, no formal sign-off needed
- "Cheers" if wrapping up a conversation
- Name only if they don't know us yet: "- Ben"
