# Record of Processing — the customer comms desk

**Controller:** Handy Services (Nottingham). **Contact:** bookings@handyservices.app
**Scope:** the customer-facing comms desk — WhatsApp, SMS, webform, inbound and outbound calls,
the quote page, and the agentic "spine" that reads a thread and drafts or sends the reply.
**Status:** LIVING DOCUMENT. **Written:** 8 September 2026 (plan v2 item 0.8, review finding s30 F7).
**Derived from the code**, not from a policy document; every row below names the file that proves it.
The customer-facing version of this is `client/src/pages/PrivacyPolicyPage.tsx` (`/privacy`).

This is a record under UK GDPR Article 30. It is not legal advice, and it has not been reviewed by
a lawyer or a DPO.

## 2. Purposes

1. Answer an enquiry and understand the job well enough to quote it.
2. Prepare, send and explain a quote; take a deposit; book the work.
3. Carry out the work and keep a record of it, including photo evidence for warranty claims.
4. Chase a thread that has gone quiet, within the messaging rules the customer has agreed to.
5. Keep the business's books and meet tax obligations.
6. Run and check the desk itself: cost per reply, run records, sampled quality review.

## 3. Lawful bases

| Purpose | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| 1–3 (quote, book, carry out) | **Contract** (Art. 6(1)(b)) — steps taken at the customer's request |
| 4 (follow-up), 6 (running and checking the desk) | **Legitimate interests** (Art. 6(1)(f)) — replying to people who contacted us, and keeping the service honest; balanced against the customer's expectation of a reply from the business they messaged |
| 5 (tax and accounts) | **Legal obligation** (Art. 6(1)(c)) |
| Call recording | **Consent**, withdrawable |
| Marketing-category WhatsApp templates and re-engagement | **Consent** / soft opt-in, with an opt-out honoured on every channel (`server/opt-out.ts`, `server/agents/comms-lanes.ts`) |

Automated decision-making: the desk drafts and may send replies without a person seeing them
first, but a person sets every price, approves every quote and confirms every booking, and
exceptions (money, complaints, refunds, callbacks, gas work) are routed to a person
(`server/spine/triage.ts`, `server/spine/decide.ts`, `server/handover.ts`). There is no
solely-automated decision producing a legal or similarly significant effect within Article 22.

## 5. Where it is stored

- **Database** — Neon Postgres (`server/db.ts`). Region not determinable from this repository.
- **Media and call recordings** — AWS S3, `AWS_REGION` defaulting to `eu-west-2` (London), objects
  private, streamed by the app rather than served from a public URL (`server/media-store.ts`).
- **Local disk on the app host** — `server/storage/media` as a cache, plus the media-description
  cache at `server/storage/media/.descriptions/`. Railway's disk is ephemeral, so this is a copy,
  not the record (`server/media-store.ts`).
- **Processors' own systems** — as above, for as long as each provider's terms allow.

## 6. How long it is kept — and the gap

The published notice states: job/quote/invoice records six years; conversations, photos and video,
and call recordings and transcripts up to two years.

**Nothing in the code enforces any of it.** As of 8 September 2026 there is no retention job, no
scheduled deletion, and no expiry column on `messages`, `calls` or the media store; a search for a
purge or retention task across `server/` and `scripts/` returns none. The `messages` table comments
say the opposite of deletion for one historical class of rows — 58,216 undelivered outbound rows
were *marked* rather than removed, on the reasoning that "customer comms are a business record"
(`shared/schema.ts`, the quarantine block). Deletion today happens only when someone runs it by
hand, or on a customer's request.

Two consequences to fix, in this order:

1. The stated two-year period is a promise the system does not keep. Either a job must carry it
   out, or the notice must say what actually happens.
2. A subject-erasure request has no tooling behind it: there is no delete-my-data path across
   `messages`, `calls`, media, S3, the description cache, `agent_runs` and the processors. It would
   be done by hand today.

Neither is changed by this document; item 0.8 describes the system, it does not alter it.

## 7. Rights, and how they are met today

Access, rectification, erasure, restriction and objection are handled by email to
bookings@handyservices.app, within one month, by hand. Opt-out of messaging is automated and
honoured across every channel and every sender (`server/opt-out.ts`, the send gate in
`server/spine/exit.ts`). Complaints go to the ICO (ico.org.uk).

## 8. Security measures, as the code shows them

- One send gate: every customer-facing message carries an approver and a run id and passes the
  exit (`server/spine/exit.ts`); the registry of senders is item 0.2's work.
- Media objects are private; the app streams them (`server/media-store.ts`).
- Admin pages are behind login; the contractor portal is behind its own session
  (`server/contractor-auth.ts`).
- Secrets are environment variables, never in the repository.
- PostHog session recording masks text inputs (`client/src/lib/posthog.ts`).

Not asserted here: encryption at rest beyond what Neon and S3 provide by default, access logging,
or a tested backup restore. None of those was verified for this document.
