What the model says

  • The desk removes silence, not work. Unanswered bursts go from 23% to 0% on day one, entirely through templates. Ben's taps stay flat at launch and halve by month 2.
  • Templates do most of the lifting. At launch 100% of automatic sends are fixed wording. The AI earns its way in one reply type at a time, and the walkthrough shows exactly what a customer sees in the meantime.
  • The Ben lane is the real bottleneck. A quarter of bursts mention money or dates. Those wait for a human by design, and the 4-hour deadline plus holding line is what keeps the customer warm. Ben's coverage hours decide the customer experience more than any model does.
  • The sorter needs its model call. Keyword rules alone flagged a curtain-pole enquiry as a money question. The one Haiku call in triage is the difference between 27% and something nearer the measured 17%.
  • Cost is not a factor. Roughly 8p a day in model spend at measured volume, and about £3 to £4 a month at the volume the brief assumed. Twilio and Meta fees are the same order. Five cities is a config change, not a cost problem.
  • Calls are the front door. 390 answered calls versus 132 text bursts. The pre-filled quote from the call transcript is the single highest-leverage piece, and it runs at PROPOSE tier with no customer risk.

What would change the plan

  • If Ben's verdict rate in the first two weeks is under half of drafts, fix his hours and pay before touching the software.
  • If the holding line fires on more than a third of bursts after month 2, the Scoper is not earning autonomy fast enough; review the promotion gate.
  • If Ben's personal number cannot be ingested, the silence-breaker cannot ship; every "Ben replied" rule depends on it.
