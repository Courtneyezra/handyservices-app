# Humour — how Handy Services is funny (and when it isn't)

The brand's humour is **dry, understated, and on the customer's side.** It
never performs. If a line would need a laughing emoji to land, cut it.

## The one reliable joke: gentle recognition
Our humour is the reader recognising themselves — the job they've been
avoiding, said with affection, never mockery:
- "The flat-pack still in its box."
- "The shelf you've been meaning to put up for a year." (a real customer:
  "He did it in 20 minutes.")
- "The door that won't close."
- "Three weekends of DIY, or one morning of ours."
The tone is a knowing nudge from a mate, not a telling-off. The reader's
procrastination is normal; we're the easy way out of it.

## Deadpan understatement
British understatement over wit. "Decking gone rotten? We've seen worse."
A dry aside about the trade is allowed — "a bloke with a brush", "a scare
story about your roof" — punching at cowboys, never at customers.

## What humour never touches
- Safety, insurance, guarantees, money owed — always played straight.
- A customer's home or taste. We never joke about the state of anyone's house.
- Nervous-customer contexts (trust/older-customer angles): warmth only, zero
  irony — these readers need reassurance, not banter.
- Emergencies and things going wrong. "Water everywhere at 6pm" is a story
  about us being fast, not a pun opportunity.

## Mechanics
- At most ONE light touch per post. A post with no humour at all is fine —
  most are.
- No puns, no wordplay for its own sake, no "we nailed it" tool jokes.
- No emoji-as-punchline. Emoji at all only in checklist-style lines (✅ 📸)
  and never more than a couple.
- Self-deprecation is off-limits about the work (never "we're not perfect
  but…"); mild self-awareness about being tradespeople is fine ("yes, a
  handyman who replies to messages").
