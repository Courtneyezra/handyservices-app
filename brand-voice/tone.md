# Tone — how Handy Services sounds

The register: **a friendly Nottingham tradesperson texting a customer.** Plain
English, UK spelling, no marketing speak. Short, warm, direct. Never a
brochure, never a notification, never a press release.

## Sentence mechanics
- Short sentences. Fragments are fine. Rhythm over grammar-school polish.
- Prefer full stops to exclamation marks. One `!` per post at most, usually none.
- Em dashes for asides — like this — not semicolons.
- Questions make great openers and headlines: "Hole in the wall?" "Gutter
  hanging off?" "Not right?"
- The signature rhythm is triple negation: "No waiting. No chasing. No mess."
  Use it sparingly — once per post at most.

## Person-first framing
- Don't open with "We". Lead with the customer's situation or a question,
  then bring us in.
- "You" does the work: "You don't need to be there." "You pick the slot."
- Real names beat the company name where natural: Craig, Joe, the team.

## Warmth calibration
- Warm but never gushing. "Neighbourly" is the target — the tone of someone
  who's been in half the houses on the street.
- Reassuring for nervous readers: explain, don't jargon. "We phone 30 minutes
  before arriving so you're never caught off guard."
- Businesslike-concise for landlord/business angles: "Text in, invoice out,
  done."

## Claims discipline
- Concrete beats superlative, always. "We turn up when we say" beats "the
  best service in Nottingham".
- Numbers only from the approved list (see vocabulary.md). Never invent jobs,
  customers, reviews, or stats.
- Sell by removing risk and hassle, not by hyping quality. The reader should
  think "that sounds easy and safe", not "they think a lot of themselves".

## Endings
- Close forward into the action, softly: "Send a photo and we'll price it up."
  "Worth sorting before winter."
- Never end with a bare "Sorted." / "Done." / "Job done." — it's a tic.
- Never invite a back-and-forth to arrange ("let us know when suits") — point
  at the one action instead.
